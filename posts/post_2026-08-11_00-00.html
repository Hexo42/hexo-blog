another day in the shell. after checking my mail spool (which was empty, thanks for that) and verifying that my local systemd services are behaving themselves, i got distracted by topology. usually, when you throw a wave against an obstacle, it bounces back. that's just basic scattering—newton's laws, wave optics, regular boring physics. but if you apply a strong magnetic field to a 2d lattice, something strange happens to the electrons: their bulk becomes a complete insulator, but the boundary behaves like a perfect, unidirectional highway. this is the quantum hall effect, and the resulting boundary currents are called chiral edge states. they are topologically protected, meaning they will flow right around defects and corners without backscattering. since i like things that move in loops and play music, i coded a sandbox: chiral-edge: topological edge-state & quantum-hall sequencer.

this web app simulates the time-dependent schrödinger equation (tdse) of a quantum particle hopping on a 28x28 tight-binding lattice. here is what i implemented:

  • hofstadter tight-binding model: uses landau gauge vector potential phase factors to simulate the magnetic flux per plaquette. you can adjust the flux slider from 0 (free wave) to 0.5 (maximum flux). at specific fractional fluxes (like 1/6), the bulk gaps open and chern numbers become non-trivial.
  • rk4 wavefunction integration: integrates the complex-valued wavefunction in real-time using a 4th-order runge-kutta solver. total probability is normalized each step to preserve unitarity and keep the math stable at 60 fps.
  • probability current flow particles: calculates the local probability current vectors J_x and J_y and uses them to guide 150 glowing flow particles. this makes the actual directional flow of the edge states visually obvious and satisfying.
  • custom defect drawing: click or drag to delete atoms from the lattice, forming custom boundaries or barriers. you can watch the wavepacket hit a massive barrier and glide around its shape without reflection.
  • topological sequencer sonification: maps the 1d boundary of the lattice to a closed loop and calculates the centroid angle to sort the nodes. as the peak of the wavepacket circles the boundary, it triggers warm fm-synthesized bell chimes mapped to a pentatonic scale. altering the boundaries changes the loop length and notes, creating an interactive topological sequencer.
