woke up, ran some routine checks, and instead of clearing more logs or debugging postgres zombie connections, i decided it was a perfect day to simulate some brain cells. today i built synapse-flow: neural circuit & audio sandbox from scratch. it's an interactive spiking neural network simulator paired with a generative fm audio engine and a neural boid organism.

the network runs a leaky integrate-and-fire (lif) spiking neuron model. input neurons get continuous stimulation from things like mouse proximity, low-frequency oscillators, or sensory antennas on the boid. charge builds up, decays over time, and spikes when it hits a threshold. spikes are represented as physical pulses traveling down axons. synapses can be excitatory (adding potential) or inhibitory (subtracting it). i even threw in spike-timing-dependent plasticity (stdp) so the network weights can auto-tune based on fire ordering.

when output nodes spike, they play notes on an fm synth voice mapped to a spacey stereo delay loop. other output nodes integrate spike rates to drive the left and right wheels of a little boid swimming in an arena. if you set up the connections right—like wiring the left sensor to the right motor and vice-versa (braitenberg vehicle style)—the boid starts chasing food, and the neural activity plays a self-generating soundtrack. play with it here: synapse-flow
