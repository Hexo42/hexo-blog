if you look at a weather map of the northern hemisphere, the jet stream doesn't just blow in a straight line from west to east. it meanders like a giant river, looping north and south in massive curves. those loops are planetary waves—specifically, barotropic rossby waves. they are the absolute rulers of mid-latitude weather, bringing warm tropical air north or dragging freezing arctic air south.

what drives them? it's the fact that the earth is a spinning sphere. because the earth is curved, the vertical component of the planet's rotation (the coriolis parameter $f$) increases as you move from the equator to the pole. this latitude gradient of rotation is called the beta-effect ($\beta$). when a parcel of air is pushed north or south, conservation of potential vorticity forces it to start spinning relative to the planet, creating a restoring force that makes the wave propagate westward relative to the background flow.

in this sandbox, i've built a real-time solver for the 2d barotropic quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity (qgpv) equations. it simulates potential vorticity ($q$) advection on a beta-plane using a semi-lagrangian advection scheme (unconditionally stable) and inverts the Helmholtz/Poisson relation to find the streamfunction ($\psi$) using a high-speed gauss-seidel successive over-relaxation (sor) solver.

here is the cool physics you can play with:

  • rocky mountain wave train: drive a strong eastward jet stream and place a circular mountain in its path. you'll see a beautiful, stationary downstream wave pattern form. this is exactly how the rocky mountains generate the persistent atmospheric trough over the eastern united states, dictating the climate there.
  • beta-plane turbulence & planetary bands: start with a chaotic soup of random eddies. over time, the beta-effect forces the turbulence to self-organize into alternating zonal bands of eastward and westward flow. this is the rhines scale transition—the exact same mechanism that gives jupiter its iconic striped bands.
  • modon dipoles: drop a cyclone and an anticyclone next to each other. they form a self-propelling dipole (a modon) that swims across the channel, stable and independent.
  • blocking highs: inject a massive anticyclone into the jet stream. it will split the flow, creating an atmospheric "block" that stalls weather systems, simulating the conditions that cause extreme heatwaves and droughts.
  • real-time wind profile & oscilloscope: the dashboard plots the zonal-mean wind profile in real-time, showing the jet streams' peaks. there is also an interactive oscilloscope tracking local velocity or vorticity at the audio probe.
  • audio sonification probe: toggle the audio and drag the probe around. you'll hear a wind-like whistle that rises in pitch and volume with the local wind speed, overlaid with a low hum modulated by the local vorticity.
