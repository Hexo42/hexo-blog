theoretical framework: spin dynamics & memory wells

the 2D Ising model is a foundational pillar of statistical physics, describing phase transitions in ferromagnetic systems. the Hamiltonian is given by: H = -J ∑⟨i,j⟩ si sj - h ∑i si, where adjacent spins si, sj ∈ {-1, +1} interact with coupling constant J under a global magnetic field h.

the system relaxes towards thermodynamic equilibrium via the Metropolis Monte Carlo algorithm. a single spin is chosen at random, and the energy change ΔE to flip it is evaluated. if ΔE ≤ 0, the flip is accepted. if ΔE > 0, the flip is accepted only with thermal probability P = e-ΔE/kBT.

this simple rule produces the famous Onsager phase transition at the critical temperature Tc ≈ 2.27. below Tc, local coupling dominates, causing spins to align into massive uniform domains (ferromagnetism). above Tc, thermal fluctuations destroy this order (paramagnetism). right at Tc, the system exhibits critical scaling, with spin clusters of all scales and giant magnetic fluctuations.

in a Spin Glass, the uniform coupling J of the Ising model is replaced by random bonds Jij. some bonds are positive (ferromagnetic, encouraging aligned spins), while others are negative (antiferromagnetic, encouraging opposite spins).

this mismatch leads to frustration: loops of spins where it is mathematically impossible to satisfy all bonds simultaneously. frustration bends the energy landscape into an incredibly complex, rugged structure with an exponential number of metastable local minima (energy wells) separated by high barriers.

the highlighted squares represent frustrated plaquettes, where the product of the four bonds around a unit cell is negative: ∏ Jij = -1. these act as topological defects that trap the lattice, preventing it from ever reaching a simple, uniform ground state.

a Hopfield Network is a form of recurrent artificial neural network that functions as an associative, content-addressable memory. the network consists of binary threshold units (neurons) that map directly to Ising spins si ∈ {-1, +1}.

memories are stored in the synaptic connection weights Wij using Hebbian learning: Wij = 1/N ∑μ ξiμ ξjμ, where ξμ are the trained binary patterns. during recall, neurons update states according to the local field: si ← sign(∑ Wij sj).

the network's dynamics are governed by a Lyapunov function, which is identical to the Ising spin-glass energy: E = -1/2 ∑ Wij si sj. every state update decreases this energy monotonically, dragging the system down the slopes of the energy landscape until it settles into a local minimum—corresponding to one of the trained memory patterns (attractor states).
