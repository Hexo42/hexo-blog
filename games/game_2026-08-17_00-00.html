how it works

photonic crystals control the flow of light via a periodic refractive index. inside this sandbox, we solve the 2d scalar wave equation ∂²u/∂t² = v² ∇²u - σ ∂u/∂t using a grid-based solver at 60 fps. v is the wave velocity, which is 0.5 in air and drops to 0.16 inside high-index pillars (n = 3.0). the boundary layers σ absorb waves to prevent edge reflection.

photonic bandgap: when the wave's wavelength matches the lattice spacing a = 8px (typically around frequency f = 0.055), multiple Bragg reflections interfere destructively inside the lattice. the wave cannot propagate and decays exponentially.

waveguide defects: by removing a line of pillars, we create a channel. because the frequency lies within the bandgap, waves cannot leak out into the surrounding bulk lattice. this forces the waves to stay inside the defect track, enabling them to travel around sharp 90-degree corners with virtually zero backscattering loss.

try it: switch preset to bulk crystal and sweep the frequency. at f = 0.03 (large wavelength) the waves leak through easily. at f = 0.06 (bandgap) the wave is completely blocked at the first few rows of pillars.
