Macro-Quantum Dynamics: The Physics

A Josephson Junction consists of two superconductors separated by a thin insulating barrier. The macroscopic wavefunctions of the two superconductors overlap, allowing cooper pairs to tunnel across without resistance up to a critical current I₀. This quantum phase difference φ governs the junction's electrical state via the two Josephson Relations:

  • DC Josephson Relation: I = I₀ sin(φ) (supercurrent is proportional to the sine of the phase difference).
  • AC Josephson Relation: V = (ℏ / 2e) dφ/dt (a voltage across the junction implies a rotating phase difference).

Resistively & Capacitively Shunted Junction (RCSJ) Model

To capture realistic behavior, we shunt the pure junction with a resistor (resistance R) and capacitor (capacitance C). Applying Kirchhoff's laws leads to a second-order stochastic differential equation identical to a particle rolling down a Tilted Washboard Potential:

βc d²φ/dt² + dφ/dt + sin(φ) = idc + iac sin(ωt) + noise(T)

Here, the McCumber parameter βc represents capacitance (mass of the particle), the linear first-derivative term represents resistance (damping), and the bias current idc tilts the potential. When idc < 1, the phase particle can remain trapped in a well (zero average voltage, zero electrical resistance). When idc > 1, the particle rolls continuously down the washboard, yielding periodic phase slips and a non-zero average voltage.

Key Phenomena to Explore

  • Hysteresis (βc > 1): Switch Damping to a value higher than 1. Auto-Sweep the bias current. Notice that the phase particle stays trapped in a well going up until idc > 1, but once rolling, it keeps rolling even as current falls far below 1. This represents the hysteretic transition of underdamped junctions.
  • Shapiro Steps: Turn up AC Amplitude iac to around 1.0 and watch the I-V curve. The phase particle locks its speed to the driving frequency, generating flat horizontal plateaus (steps) at discrete average voltages <v> = nω. This is the quantum standard for defining the Volt.
  • DC SQUID (Superconducting Quantum Interference Device): Two junctions in parallel inside a superconducting loop are sensitive to magnetic flux Φext. Watch how the total critical current modulates periodically, demonstrating macro-quantum interference. Hover and drag the magnet in SQUID mode to see the circulating current J and voltage shift.
  • 1D coupled Chain: A discrete chain of junctions coupled inductively. A phase slip at one end creates a magnetic soliton (fluxon) that slides through the array, showing how collective phase modes propagate in superconducting arrays.
