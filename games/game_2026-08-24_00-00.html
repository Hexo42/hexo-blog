The Joukowsky Transform is a conformal mapping defined in the complex plane as $z = \zeta + \frac{c^2}{\zeta}$ (here $c=1$). It maps a perfect cylinder (circle) in the mathematical $\zeta$-plane to an aerodynamic airfoil shape in the physical $z$-plane.

By moving the circle's center ($\zeta_c = \mu_x + i\mu_y$) away from the origin:

  • $\mu_x < 0$ (Center X) controls the thickness of the wing. Moving it left rounds the leading edge.
  • $\mu_y$ (Center Y) controls the camber (curvature) of the wing. Positive values curve it upward.

In potential flow, the fluid can slip past the airfoil boundary with zero viscosity. However, at the sharp trailing edge, a mathematical singularity arises: the fluid must wrap around the sharp corner at infinite speed, which is physically impossible.

The Kutta Condition states that the fluid must leave the trailing edge smoothly. To achieve this, the flow must develop exactly the right amount of circulation (vorticity) $\Gamma = 4\pi R U_\infty \sin(\alpha - \beta)$. This circulation pushes the rear stagnation point exactly onto the sharp trailing edge, eliminating the singularity.

Uncheck "Enforce Kutta Condition" to see what happens when the stagnation point drifts off the trailing edge and the flow is forced to wrap around the tip!

By adding circulation $\Gamma$, the flow speed on top of the airfoil increases, while the flow speed underneath decreases. According to Bernoulli's Equation ($P + \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 = \text{const}$):

  • Higher speed on top yields lower pressure (suction).
  • Lower speed underneath yields higher pressure.

This pressure differential creates Lift. The Kutta-Joukowski theorem states the lift per unit span is $L = \rho U_\infty \Gamma$. In potential flow, there is no viscosity, so Drag is exactly zero (d'Alembert's Paradox).
